# Review a dictated product note before it becomes a task

A rough product note can contain three different things: an observation, your explanation for it, and a possible change. Dictating them together is convenient; treating the whole paragraph as an agreed requirement can lose that distinction.

This guide is for people who move between user conversations and implementation. That combination of responsibilities is described in the [product-builder role](https://closedloop.sh/blog/what-is-a-product-builder). Here, the practical job is smaller: review one dictated note before handing it to someone else or turning it into a task.

Use a text field that works with your dictation setup. The review below is manual; it does not require a cleanup rule or assume the app can verify what happened.

## Try a short read-back exercise

Use this fictional practice note:

> Someone said the date picker was confusing. I think they expected Monday to be the first day. Maybe we should change the default, but I need to check which screen they meant.

Read it once and label each clause:

| Clause | What it contributes | What must survive your review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Someone said the date picker was confusing.” | Reported observation | The person, screen and exact wording are unspecified. |
| “I think they expected Monday to be the first day.” | Interpretation | The expectation has not been confirmed. |
| “Maybe we should change the default.” | Possible response | This is an option, not a decision. |
| “I need to check which screen they meant.” | Follow-up | The missing location still needs an answer. |

Do not repair the note into “Users need Monday as the default.” That changes one unspecified report into a general requirement and removes the uncertainty.

## Compare with a conservative repair

Keep the meaning while making the note easier to use:

- **Observation:** A person reported that a date picker was confusing. The screen and exact wording are not recorded.
- **Interpretation:** They may have expected Monday as the first day of the week. This needs confirmation.
- **Option:** Consider a different default if the expectation is confirmed.
- **Next question:** Which screen were they using, and what did they expect to happen?

The repair is useful because someone else can see what is known and what still needs checking. It does not establish that changing the default is the right response.

## Reuse the structure on your next note

After dictating, read the text and fill four short lines:

- **Observation:** What was actually reported or noticed?
- **Interpretation:** What explanation am I adding?
- **Option:** What change am I considering?
- **Next question:** What would resolve the most important unknown?

If a field has no answer, leave the uncertainty visible. Keep words such as “maybe,” “I think,” and “not sure” attached to the statement they qualify.

Before creating a task, compare the reviewed note with your own source notes. Check names, dates, quantities and the final clause, then decide what belongs in the task and what belongs in a follow-up question. Clear wording should make that decision easier without making it for you.
